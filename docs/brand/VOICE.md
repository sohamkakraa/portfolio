# Voice

How sohamkakra.com sounds. The *why* is in [`PHILOSOPHY.md`](./PHILOSOPHY.md). This page is the *how*.

Status: **draft**. Last reviewed: **21 Oct 2026**.

---

## Voice stays, tone moves

**The voice never changes** (fixed). One person talking to a friend: warm, curious, a little funny, never preachy. Humour is welcome, but clarity comes first. If a joke makes a sentence harder to understand, cut the joke.

**The tone changes with the moment** (flexible). An essay about a storm and an error message shouldn't sound the same, but they should sound like the same person.

| Place | Tone | Sounds like | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home and the sky narration | Wonder, slow, short lines | "Nothing about the storm had changed. I had." | A sales pitch |
| Journeys (trip essays) | Honest, personal, a little raw | "A raw essay about travelling alone, learning to dive, and doing things while scared." | A travel brochure |
| Library (learnings) | A friend explaining over tea | One idea, one real-life comparison, one source | A lecture |
| Research | Clear, precise, curious | What the question is, what was tried, what was learned | Buzzwords, hype |
| About | Warm, a little playful | "The things I figured out on a bus." | A list of adjectives |
| Come along and forms | Inviting, simple | "Tell me if you'd like to come along." | Pressure, urgency |
| Errors and empty states | Calm, useful, short | "That didn't send. Check your connection and try again." | Blame, "Oops!" |
| Consent and privacy | Plain, honest, complete | Says exactly what is recorded and why | Legal fog, guilt |
| Alt text | Factual, visual | "Snow-covered Himalayan peaks under a sky full of stars, with a few village lights in the valley below" | "Image of…", "Beautiful view" |
| Link previews (meta descriptions) | One clear sentence about the page | "Every trip so far, from Kenya in 2021 to the Alps in 2026." | Keyword lists |
| Résumé and CV | Formal, factual | Results and numbers | Jokes, cosmic words |

The résumé is the one exception to the word list below: recruiters' search tools look for "AI", so it may appear there as a plain keyword.

## How we write

All fixed.

- **Short sentences, one idea each.** If a sentence needs a second breath, make it two.
- **Plain punctuation.** Full stops, commas and brackets. No em dashes and no semicolons.
- **"I" for Soham, "you" for the visitor.** Never "we" on Soham's own pages, never "users".
- **British spelling:** colour, travelling, programme, summarise.
- **Sentence case** for headings and buttons: "Write to me", not "Write To Me".
- **Dates:** "21 Oct 2026" in lists and labels, "October 2026" in prose.
- **Places named plainly:** "Hampi, India". Coordinates live in the X-ray layer.
- **Hindi** only for words and quotes that are Hindi, in italics, with an English translation beside them.
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one per page. No emoji in copy.
- **Links say where they go:** "Read the Barcelona essay", never "click here".

## Words

| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AI | What it does: "agents", "a model that sorts tickets" | A fashion word that says nothing about the work |
| Cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, revolutionary, innovative, seamless, robust | What is better, and by how much | Everyone says it, so it means nothing |
| Leverage, utilise | Use | Plainer |
| Passionate about | Show it with a story | Telling is weaker than showing |
| Content | The actual thing: photos, essay, list | "Content" is a container, not a thing |
| Users | Visitors, people, you | People, not data points |
| Submit | What happens: "Send", "Save", "Join" | Says what the button does |
| Click here, read more | Where the link goes | Works for screen readers and skimmers |
| Oops, something went wrong | What happened and what to do next | Useful, not cute |
| Simply, just, easy (in instructions) | Leave it out | It isn't easy for everyone |
| Clusters, nebulae, transmit (as menu labels) | Journeys, Research, Write to me | See [Finding your way](./PHILOSOPHY.md#finding-your-way) |

**Words that sound like us:** come along, write to me, so far, still figuring out, made, wander.

## This, not that

Real sentences from the site, and how they would read in our voice.

### 1. Say what you do, not what you are

> **Before** (work page description): "AI engineer and Talent Lab resident at Prosus. Roles, degrees, projects and CV."
>
> **After:** "Researcher and Talent Lab resident at Prosus, studying how agent systems are built. Roles, degrees, projects and CV."

"AI engineer" is a label from the word list, and it no longer describes the work.

### 2. Swap the tagline for a real sentence

> **Before** (about subtitle): "Data Science and AI builder with a product-first systems mindset."
>
> **After:** "I study how agent systems are built, what holds them together and what pulls them apart."

The second one is Soham's own sentence. Anyone could have written the first.

### 3. Replace abstract claims with something that happened

> **Before** (about section): "Readable. Turning complex model behavior into interfaces people can trust."
>
> **After:** "Built a test that compared six ways of answering questions across 1,120 tickets, checked by code and by two model judges."

The pattern: what was built, for whom, and what it changed. Facts from real work only.

### 4. Shorter, with nothing left out

> **Before** (consent banner): "May I use analytics to see how you found this site and move through it? This records page views, scroll progress, clicks, basic device details and campaign labels, and shares anonymous usage data with Google Analytics so I can improve the writing, SEO and experience. Google's advertising features are switched off. No ads, marketing profiles or cross-site tracking."
>
> **After:** "May I count your visit? It records page views, scrolling, clicks, basic device details and campaign labels, and shares anonymous usage data with Google Analytics. I use it to make the writing and the site better. Google's advertising features are off. No ads, no profiles, no tracking across other sites."

Every fact stays. The long middle sentence becomes two, and "SEO" goes because most visitors don't know it.

### Already right, keep doing this

- "That didn't send. Check your connection and try again." Says what happened and what to do, in nine words.
- "Where I've been, and where I want to go next. Tell me if you'd like to come along." Warm, and ends with an open door.
- "The bucket list, the questions I'm still figuring out (send me your guess), and the things I figured out on a bus." Specific, playful, and invites a reply.
- "Nothing about the storm had changed. I had." Vast and small in eight words.

## Before publishing

Read the text out loud once, then check:

1. Would Soham say this to a friend?
2. Does it teach or show one thing?
3. Is every word on the avoid list gone?
4. Does it end with somewhere to go next?
5. Has Soham read and approved it? (Required for anything proposed by automation.)
